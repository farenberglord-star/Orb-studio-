🏦 BANK TARIFFS · MACHINE-READABLE

Account fees, deposit rates and loan terms are hidden by banks in PDFs and small print. Ready-made cards of the kind {"rate":3.5,"fee":0} hardly exist, so neither a person nor an AI agent can honestly compare offers. Here is the bridge: we read the official documents of banks in Germany and the US ourselves and publish them as clean cards with a source and a check date.

HOW THE BRIDGE WORKS

  1. Once a week – automatically.
    Claude opens the banks’ official documents itself: in Germany the Entgeltinformation that banks must publish under the payment accounts act (ZKG), in the US the fee schedule and rate pages.
  2. Official domains only.
    Copies on other sites are often out of date (for example an ING price list from 2020 with a 700 € threshold), so we do not use them.
  3. No certainty – no number.
    If a document does not open or the figures look odd, the card keeps the previous value and gets a status instead of a guess.
  4. US average rate – every 12 hours.
    The FDIC series (SNDR, SNRC) are fetched by the Orb Studio server itself, without human involvement.

Information, not financial advice. Bank terms change; check them with the bank via the “source” link before opening an account.

About this page in brief · updated 2026-11-08

Where do the bank tariffs on the page come from?

From official bank documents (Germany: Entgeltinformation/ZKG; US: fee schedules) plus the average FDIC rate. Every card has a status, a check date and a link to the source.

What does the status blocked or check mean?

“check” – the source gives no date; “blocked” – the bank blocks the document from automatic reading and the figure is not published.
