Baird sensor

A number without a source, a time and a unit is a rumour. The sensor gives every number the site's sensors publish a passport: where it comes from, when it was measured, whether it is stale, which unit, and whether it lies in a plausible range. It also reaches the same quantity by two independent routes: if they disagree, a rate, a unit or a time is wrong somewhere.

    In honour of Kenneth Baird (1923–2022)

    Kenneth Baird was a Canadian physicist and metrologist at the National Research Council of Canada. He built stabilised lasers and laser length standards and took part in the work that led to the 1983 redefinition of the metre through the speed of light. The core rule of metrology is traceability: a measurement counts only with an unbroken chain to the standard. Wikipedia article.

    The sensor is named in his honour; Orb Studio is not connected with Kenneth Baird, his family or the National Research Council of Canada.
