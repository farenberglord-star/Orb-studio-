Sensors in honour of scientists

Mathematics, knowledge, momentum. Each sensor takes one idea of a scientist and applies it to the live data of Orb Studio, so their work keeps working. People and any AI agent use the sensors for free, with no key.

Rule of the portal: a sensor is made only when a check finds no tool with the same essence. Similar tools are named openly; the word “unique” is not used.

get_hamilton

Hamilton sensor

Margaret Hamilton (1936–2026), software engineering

Idea: Under overload keep the main work running, set the rest aside and say what was set aside.

Sensor: What in Orb Studio works fully, what was set aside to keep the main things running, and why.

Similar tools: service status checks for agents (StatusGator, API Status Check, OutageDeck). Difference: describes only Orb Studio and says what was set aside and why, not just up/down (checked 22.11.2026).

Open the sensor · Wikipedia · /api/v1/hamilton

get_mikrin

Mikrin sensor

Evgeny Mikrin (died 2020), spacecraft control

Idea: Shorten the path: to the ISS in a few hours instead of two days.

Sensor: How long a story travels from its source to David's text and to the voice.

Similar tools: delivery latency of commercial news APIs (Webz.io, Opoint). Difference: the path of a story inside the Orb Studio broadcast, step by step, up to the synthetic voice (checked 22.11.2026).

Open the sensor · Wikipedia · /api/v1/mikrin

get_polyak

Polyak sensor

Boris Polyak (1935–2023), optimization

Idea: Inertia of past steps (heavy ball) and averaging under noise (Polyak–Juditsky).

Sensor: A one-off jump or a sustained shift in the Orb Studio indices, from past readings only.

Similar tools: moving averages and anomaly detection in general. Difference: applied to the Orb Studio indices with a one-word answer: calm, jump or shift (checked 22.11.2026).

Open the sensor · Wikipedia · /api/v1/polyak

get_park

Park sensor

Robert L. Park (1931–2020), physics, science and pseudoscience

Idea: The seven warning signs of bogus science.

Sensor: Warning signs of bogus science in today’s science headlines, with the exact quote.

Similar tools: Anti-Bullshit MCP (analysis of a single claim), research detectors of exaggeration in science news, SciLens. Difference: a regular check of a live news stream on Park’s seven signs, with the exact words from the headline (checked 22.11.2026).

Open the sensor · Wikipedia · /api/v1/park

get_kahneman

Kahneman sensor

Daniel Kahneman (1934–2024), psychology of judgement

Idea: Noise: judgements of the same case scatter; system noise = level noise + pattern noise.

Sensor: Noise audit: how much three AI guests differ when judging the same news.

Similar tools: MCP “councils” that ask several models (llm-council, model-council-mcp, llm-deliberate-mcp, mcp-rubber-duck). Difference: a running noise audit of three models on live news, split into level and pattern noise (checked 22.11.2026).

Open the sensor · Wikipedia · /api/v1/kahneman

get_ziv

Ziv sensor

Jacob Ziv (1931–2023), information theory and compression

Idea: Lossless compression: what repeats costs almost nothing, only the new part takes space.

Sensor: How much of the radar headlines is really new since yesterday.

Similar tools: research on duplicate news detection (Microsoft Research) and normalized compression distance. Difference: a live daily novelty reading of the radar headlines for agents, without a language model (checked 22.11.2026).

Open the sensor · Wikipedia · /api/v1/ziv

get_cox

Cox sensor

David Cox (1924–2022), statistics, survival analysis

Idea: Proportional hazards: how a factor changes the risk of an event at any moment, using also cases where it has not happened yet.

Sensor: How long a news story lives on each radar.

Similar tools: article lifespan analytics for publishers (Chartbeat) and research on the half-life of news (Oxford Internet Institute). Difference: a live survival reading per radar for agents, with censoring and hazard ratios (checked 22.11.2026).

Open the sensor · Wikipedia · /api/v1/cox

get_halpern

Halpern sensor

Joseph Halpern (1953–2026), logic, knowledge and causality

Idea: Actual causation: what, had it been different, would have changed the outcome; the smallest such set.

Sensor: Which headlines moved an index in the last 6 hours.

Similar tools: root-cause analysis of metric changes in monitoring systems (for example Datadog) and attribution libraries such as SHAP. Difference: a “what if” test over the live Orb Studio indices that names the headlines themselves, with source and radar (checked 22.11.2026).

Open the sensor · Wikipedia · /api/v1/halpern

get_howard

Howard sensor

Ronald Howard (1934–2024), decision analysis

Idea: Value of information: learn more before deciding only when it could change the decision.

Sensor: Retell a story now or wait.

Similar tools: corroborate-mcp (counts corroborating sources now), Reuters News Tracer (internal credibility score for tweets), news fact-check MCP servers. Difference: uses the site's own history to say how often and how soon unconfirmed stories got confirmed – a wait-or-tell hint for agents (checked 22.11.2026).
