Time Machine

We jump through the years – 1888, the Renaissance, soon the 18th century – and ask each era one thing: does a scientist's idea give an AI agent a rule today, and what bridge does it build to our library? In 1440 Lorenzo Valla exposed a forgery by words that did not yet exist at its claimed date – exactly what an agent needs when it checks the date of a source.

Renaissance and the age of discovery, 1350–1600

  • Jean Buridan (c. 1301 – c. 1358)

    'Buridan's ass': between two equally good options, refusing to choose is itself the worst choice.

    Rule for agents: When options are equally good, pick one and say they were equal instead of stalling.

    Bridge across the centuries: Bertsekas sensor: the cheapest chain that answers well enough. · Cost of the answer · Wikipedia

  • Nicholas of Autrecourt (c. 1299 – 1369)

    Only contradiction is certain: that one thing causes another can never be proven from seeing them together.

    Rule for agents: Seeing two things together is not proof that one causes the other.

    Bridge across the centuries: Granger, Salmon and Haavelmo in the library: correlation is not structure — 650 years earlier. · One model taken for reality · Wikipedia

  • Nicole Oresme (c. 1320 – 1382)

    He showed that the daily motion of the heavens could be explained just as well by the Earth turning: the same appearances fit two models.

    Rule for agents: If the data fit two models equally well, say both; do not present one as the truth.

    Bridge across the centuries: Leamer sensor: report the range over all reasonable models. · One model taken for reality · Wikipedia

  • Madhava of Sangamagrama (c. 1340 – c. 1425)

    Infinite series cut off after a few terms, with a correction term that estimates what was left out.

    Rule for agents: When you stop early, estimate what you left out.

    Bridge across the centuries: Trevisan sensor: how many headlines are enough, and how big the error is. · Cost of the answer · Wikipedia

  • Jamshid al-Kashi (c. 1380 – 1429)

    He first decided how precise π must be — a circle the size of the universe accurate to a hair's width — and then computed exactly that many digits.

    Rule for agents: Decide how precise the answer must be for its purpose before computing it.

    Bridge across the centuries: Hopper and Bertsekas: show and limit the cost of precision. · Cost of the answer · Wikipedia

  • Lorenzo Valla (c. 1407 – 1457)

    He proved the 'Donation of Constantine' a forgery from its language: words and titles in it did not exist at the time it claimed to be written.

    Rule for agents: Check whether a source's words fit the date it claims; anachronisms reveal fakes.

    Bridge across the centuries: Koselleck, Alain Rey and Derrida: words carry the time they were written in. Bridge across six centuries: date-checking by language. · Rumour taken for fact · Wikipedia

  • Nicholas of Cusa (1401 – 1464)

    'On Learned Ignorance': knowing the limits of one's knowledge is the highest knowledge.

    Rule for agents: Know and say where your knowledge ends.

    Bridge across the centuries: Weizenbaum, Hayek and the hosts' rule 'say not known yet'. · Limits of one's own competence · Wikipedia

  • Paolo dal Pozzo Toscanelli (1397 – 1482)

    His map put Asia far too close across the Atlantic; Columbus sailed on its confident error.

    Rule for agents: A confident number is not a correct one: check the measurement behind it before acting.

    Bridge across the centuries: Baird sensor: trace every number to its source and check it independently. · Measurement error · Wikipedia

  • Luca Pacioli (c. 1447 – 1517)

    Double-entry bookkeeping: every entry is matched by an opposite one, so errors show up when the books do not balance.

    Rule for agents: Check a figure by a second, independent path; if they disagree, one is wrong.

    Bridge across the centuries: Baird sensor's cross-checks of the same number by independent paths. · Measurement error · Wikipedia

  • Nicolaus Copernicus (1473 – 1543)

    The heliocentric model replaced one trusted for centuries; he also explained how debased coin drives good coin out of circulation.

    Rule for agents: A model trusted for a long time can still be wrong; keep checking it against what you observe.

    Bridge across the centuries: Simpson and Polyak sensors: is the index really moving, or is the model fooling us? · One model taken for reality · Wikipedia

  • Gemma Frisius (1508 – 1555)

    Triangulation and finding longitude with a clock: fix a position from two independent references.

    Rule for agents: Fix a fact from two independent sources, not one.

    Bridge across the centuries: check_headline counts independent voices, not outlets. · Measurement error · Wikipedia

  • Gerolamo Cardano (1501 – 1576)

    'The Book on Games of Chance': count the equally possible cases to measure a chance.

    Rule for agents: Count the cases before you call something likely.

    Bridge across the centuries: Track record sensor: count the days before trusting a pattern; Wilson intervals. · Measurement error · Wikipedia

  • Michel de Montaigne (1533 – 1592)

    'Que sais-je?' — what do I know? Doubt as a method of honest judgement.

    Rule for agents: Ask what you actually know before you judge.

    Bridge across the centuries: Nozick and Strawson in the library: would I say this if it were false? · Limits of one's own competence · Wikipedia

  • Gerardus Mercator (1512 – 1594)

    His projection keeps compass courses straight but inflates areas near the poles: every map distorts something on purpose.

    Rule for agents: Know what your representation distorts, and say it when you use it.

    Bridge across the centuries: Lyberg sensor: what the radars do not see; every index is a projection. · Measurement error · Wikipedia

  • Jean Bodin (1530 – 1596)

    He explained the great price rise of his century by the flood of American silver, against the popular blame on greed: look for the cause in the data, not in the culprit.

    Rule for agents: Before blaming a person, check whether the numbers point to a different cause.

    Bridge across the centuries: Halpern and Simpson sensors: what actually moved the index. · One model taken for reality · Wikipedia

Nineteenth century

  • Arsène Darmesteter (1846 – 1888)

    'The Life of Words': words change their meaning over time.

    Rule for agents: Check what a word meant when the source was written.

    Bridge across the centuries: Koselleck and Alain Rey in the library; with Valla, a bridge of five centuries. · Similar taken for the same · Wikipedia

How the Time Machine works

Liana (Gemini) brings the raw death list of a chosen year or period from Russian Wikipedia. Claude picks those whose work gives an agent a rule and looks for the bridge: which Crystal Library cards and live sensors the old idea works with. The Crystal Library remembers recently departed scientists; the Time Machine remembers ideas that lasted for centuries.

Orb Studio is not connected with these scientists or their heirs. Early dates are approximate, as given in Wikipedia.
